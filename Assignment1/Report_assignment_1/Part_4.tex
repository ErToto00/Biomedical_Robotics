\section{Part 4: Target Reaching Performance Analysis}

This part performs a reaching analysis on a set of targets, with the objective to evaluate the effectiveness and the precision of the cursor control during the experiment. The necessary times to reach the targets and the number of trajectory corrections (overshoots) are analysed. 

\subsection{Target selection}
Here, targets that must be included in the analysis are selected. In particular, only the targets that were reached are considered and, in the center-out case, central targets are excluded in order to consider only outwards movements. Instead, in the random task, all the reached targets are considered. 

\subsection{Performance analysis}
Once the target are selected, parameters are extracted:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Reach time: time spent to reach each target, expressed in seconds.
    \item Overshoots: number of "target exceeding" phenomenons or corrections performed during the target reaching phase.
\end{itemize}

The two parameters are shown here graphically:

\begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Images/Our data/reach time and overshoots.png}
        \caption{Reach times and overshoot graphics 
        }
\end{figure}

The upper plot shows the reach time for each of the 24 selected targets. The results exhibit considerable variability, with values ranging approximately from 2.5 to 19 seconds. The first reach presents the longest completion time, while subsequent reaches generally require less time, although no consistent decreasing trend can be observed.

The lower plot shows the number of overshoots for each reach. Most targets are reached without overshoots, while only a few reaches present one overshoot. No cases with more than one overshoot are observed.

Overall, the results indicate that reaching times are relatively variable, whereas the number of overshoots remains generally low. Although there is no clear and continuous improvement throughout the session, the results suggest relatively stable cursor control, with most targets being reached without requiring additional corrections.
